# Scientific context

repository role: analysis

agent role: chemical engineering thermodynamics

## Question and intended use

How closely does the unchanged 1945 Antoine correlation for pure n-heptane
reproduce independent 2026 vapor-pressure measurements over 303.2–318.2 K?
This is a small teaching study of source selection, unit conversion, physical
validation, and honest reporting. It is not a process-design property package.
Issue #36 supplies the scope; routine scientific choices are delegated to the
implementer by the task instruction.

## Equation VP-1 and domain

Willingham, Taylor, Pignocco, and Rossini (1945), equation (2), pp. 233–234,
and Table 3, p. 239, n-Heptane row (eighth paraffin row), give

\[
\log_{10}(p/\mathrm{mmHg})=A-\frac{B}{C+t/{}^\circ\mathrm C},
\quad A=6.90342,\ B=1268.636,\ C=216.951.
\]

The coefficients use the numerical Celsius temperature and mmHg pressure;
they must not be used directly with kelvin or kPa. The source's stated
measurement range is 25.9–99.3 °C and 48–780 mmHg. No parameters are fitted
here. `inputs/model.json` owns the coefficient and conversion snapshot;
`scripts/run.py` implements VP-1, and `tests/test_heptane_example.py` in the
plugin repository checks the complete comparison against measurements.

Conversions are explicit: `t/°C = T/K - 273.15` and
`p/kPa = (p/mmHg) × 133.3224 / 1000`. NIST SP 811, Section 4.2.1.1,
defines the temperature offset; Appendix B.8, M, gives the conventional mmHg
factor, rounded as printed. Input temperatures and pressures remain in their
published K and kPa. The model's historical centigrade scale is treated as
numerically Celsius; no historical temperature-scale correction is claimed.

The input preserves all five heptane rows of Busch et al. (2026), Table 2,
PDF p. 8 (lettered H). Row 1 at 298.2 K is below the correlation's stated
range and is excluded by temperature, before comparison. Rows 2–5 supply all
four in-domain points. The source describes a nominal pure component; Table 1,
heptane row, reports supplier purity ≥0.998 g g⁻¹ and explains that supplier
GC% is interpreted as mass fraction. We make no mole-fraction conversion and
no purity correction. Pure liquid and its equilibrium vapor are assumed, with
no noncondensable gas contribution or mixture effects.

## Metric and tolerance (decision for this example)

Residual means model minus measurement in kPa. Report every residual, the
maximum absolute residual, root mean square residual, and mean absolute
relative residual (denominator: measured pressure). The result-level test uses
**maximum absolute residual ≤0.55 kPa**, derived below from the stated uncertainties without using the residuals.

Table 2 footnote a reports expanded uncertainties (coverage factor k=2):
U(p)=0.3 kPa and U(T)=0.115 K + 0.002|t/°C| K. The printed values have
0.1 K and 0.1 kPa resolution, so allow another 0.05 K and 0.05 kPa for
rounding. VP-1 has derivative `dp/dT = p ln(10) B/(C+t)²`; its maximum is
less than 0.66 kPa/K on the selected interval including temperature uncertainty.
The conservative sum `0.3 + 0.05 + 0.66 × (0.206 + 0.05) = 0.51896 kPa`
rounds upward to 0.55 kPa. This is a teaching screening allowance, not a
confidence interval, parameter uncertainty estimate, or industrial standard.
No model-parameter covariance is supplied or inferred. A limit failure must
be reported and diagnosed, never removed by fitting or relaxing the limit.

The one test is retained because a pressure-unit factor error, kelvin/Celsius
error, or wrong VP-1 equation corrupts the physical comparison. Its oracle is
independent experimental pressure, not output generated by VP-1. Small model
errors below the screening allowance can escape it. No solver, discretization,
optimization, convergence campaign, or per-function tests are needed for this
direct algebraic evaluation. Finite positive results are required.

## Claim boundaries and review

This comparison is physical validation against independent experiments in
the stated interval, with an implementation behavior check. It does not
establish general code verification, a measurement uncertainty model,
parameter independence from all historical experiments, accuracy outside the
four states, mixture equilibrium, or suitability for equipment design.
The 2026 pressure measurements use sensors, not the Antoine correlation
(Section 2.2.2); its Raman composition calibration is outside this study.
The coefficients predate these experiments. The modern authors also compare
their data with a related NIST correlation; agreement is not a new discovery.

Both primary papers were opened and read in full during preparation; table
transcriptions were checked against rendered PDF pages. Independent review is
pending with the orchestrator. The notebook has not been promoted for manuscript
use. Unknown parameter covariance, historical temperature-scale effects, raw
instrument records, and international rights beyond the recorded licence
findings remain outside the claims.
