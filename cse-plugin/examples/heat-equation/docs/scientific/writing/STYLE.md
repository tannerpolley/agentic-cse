# Scientific writing style

Lead with the equation, method, metric, retained result, or limitation. Write
for a reader reproducing a numerical-verification tutorial.

Distinguish:

- observation: what a retained value, plot, or check shows;
- calculation: how the value was obtained;
- interpretation: what mechanism explains the observation;
- limitation: what the evidence cannot establish.

Use theta, xi, Fo, Delta xi, Delta Fo, and r consistently. Call the analytic
field the continuous reference, the exact-in-time centered-space field the
semidiscrete reference, and the one-step factors discrete amplification
oracles. Do not merge those roles.

Every numerical claim names its final Fo, grid or time-step sequence,
diffusion number when relevant, metric, reference, tolerance, and numerical
uncertainty. Cite Crank and Nicolson with key
`crankPracticalMethodNumerical1947` only for claims supported by the exact
source locator.

No em dashes are used in project prose. Prefer commas, parentheses, colons, or
separate sentences.
